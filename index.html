Why this exists

Surveillance infrastructure doesn't arrive as tyranny — it arrives as convenience. A camera for traffic safety. A database for efficiency. Every regime in modern history that consolidated total control did it gradually, and every one of them relied on the same raw material: knowing where people were, who they talked to, and what they did, all the time. Automated license plate readers — a 120,000+ camera network and growing — and the data centers built to store everything they capture are exactly that kind of infrastructure. Not because any specific operator intends to misuse it today, but because once permanent, searchable tracking exists, the only thing standing between surveillance and control is who gets to use it next. History doesn't prove any one system will be abused. It proves that infrastructure like this never stays neutral for long.

Client-side photo forensics — v0.1 demo

The Truth Button

Drop in a photo. It checks the file for signs of AI generation or heavy editing — no upload to a server, everything runs in your browser.

How to use this site ▾
  1. Pick a mode from the tab row below — Photo, Music/Audio, Video, Flock Cameras, Data Centers, Social Listen, Reviews, or Peptide Calc.
  2. Photo / Audio / Video: drop a file onto the box, or tap it to choose one from your device. Everything is analyzed locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded.
  3. Got a link instead of a file? Paste it into the "File a report" bar and hit Analyze Link, or use "Capture From Screen" to grab what's on your screen (with your browser's normal share-screen permission).
  4. Flock Cameras / Data Centers: use the search fields to look up nearby surveillance cameras or data center facilities.
  5. Social Listen: paste a link to have it read back to you in the terminal-style log.
  6. Reviews: browse existing reviews by category, or sign in to leave your own.
  7. Peptide Calc: enter your values in the calculator fields to get dosing results.
  8. After an analysis, check the score gauge and breakdown for the alteration signal and what drove it.
This is a heuristic demo, not a certified detector — treat results as data points, not verdicts. See the full disclaimer at the bottom of the Photo panel for details.

What this actually is: a heuristic demo, not a trained detector. It runs four independent checks: error-level analysis (does this region's compression match the rest of the photo), texture uniformity (is grain suspiciously even), a 2D frequency-spectrum analysis (do GAN/diffusion upsampling artifacts show up as periodic bumps in the frequency domain), and a Content Credentials (C2PA) presence check on the original file bytes. All four also fire on ordinary things — screenshots, re-saved JPEGs, heavy filters, or repetitive real textures like fences and fabric weaves — so treat the combined score as a set of data points, not a verdict. A production version of this tool would pair these checks with a classifier trained on known generator outputs (services like Hive or Reality Defender) for real accuracy, rather than heuristics alone.

Find Flock / ALPR cameras near you

There's no official public database of Flock Safety camera locations — Flock doesn't publish one, and has even sent a cease-and-desist to the main volunteer mapping project over it (EFF pushed back on First Amendment grounds). What exists is community-crowdsourced data. Pick a state to jump to real, live reporting.

🚗 Have I Been Flocked?

A different kind of check — this searches real FOIA-obtained Flock audit logs by license plate, to see if a search was ever run against your plate. It's independent, doesn't log what you enter, and is covered by the ACLU's "Get The Flock Out" toolkit. The dataset is incomplete (few agencies release these logs easily), and a hit doesn't necessarily mean you were stopped or investigated — just that someone with system access ran a search.

Don't see your camera documented? Report it yourself.

If a camera in your area isn't on the map, you can add it to the real, live crowdsourced record — the same one these lookups pull from. This is exactly the kind of citizen documentation EFF defended in court as protected speech.

  1. Note the cross streets or address, and which direction the camera faces.
  2. Submit it to DeFlock (feeds the live public map used above), or send it as a tip to DeFlock The USA for their reporting team.
  3. If you want it in EFF's official law-enforcement-agency tracker too, submit through the Atlas of Surveillance.

Being straight about this: The Truth Button doesn't run its own camera database or backend — this page links out to real, independently-run public-interest projects (DeFlock, DeFlock The USA, EFF's Atlas of Surveillance) rather than making up data. Their coverage is crowdsourced and incomplete, so absence from these maps doesn't mean there's no camera there — just that no one's documented it yet.

Reconstitution & Dose Calculator

This is a math tool, not medical advice — it converts a dose you already have (from a prescription, protocol, or product label) into a volume you can measure. It doesn't tell you what dose to take. Follow your prescriber's or product's dosing instructions, and talk to a licensed healthcare provider before using any peptide — many sold as "research use only" aren't evaluated by the FDA for human use.

Being straight about this: this calculator does simple arithmetic (concentration = amount ÷ volume; dose volume = dose ÷ concentration; syringe units = dose volume × syringe scale). It can't catch a typo, a wrong vial size, or a unit mix-up — always read the result back against your prescription or protocol before drawing anything into a syringe, and if a number looks off, don't use it. This tool has no idea what peptide you're calculating for and makes no claim about safety, efficacy, or appropriate dosing for any substance.
