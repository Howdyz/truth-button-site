Why conversion happens on your device

Browsers only record video natively as WebM, not MP4. Rather than upload your recording to a server to convert it — which would mean your screen recording briefly leaving your device — Thunder Video Downloader runs a real video encoder (ffmpeg, compiled to WebAssembly) directly in your browser. It's slower than a server with dedicated hardware would be, so it works best for short clips at 720p or lower — 5 minutes or under, no higher than 720p. Longer or higher-resolution recordings take proportionally longer, but nothing about what you recorded ever has to leave your computer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I record a browser tab, window, or my screen?

Open Thunder Video Downloader, click the share button, then pick the tab, window, or screen in your browser's own sharing dialog — check "Share tab audio" or "Share system audio" if you want sound in the recording. Once the source is ready, click Start Recording.

What format does it save as?

MP4 (H.264 video, AAC audio), converted locally in your browser right after you stop recording — no server-side conversion, and no separate app or upload step.

Does this upload my recording anywhere?

No. Recording and MP4 conversion both happen locally in your browser (the conversion uses ffmpeg compiled to WebAssembly). Nothing is sent to a server — the file only exists on your device until you download it.

Why does the MP4 conversion take a while?

Browsers only record video as WebM natively, so converting to MP4 requires real video encoding, done entirely on your own device rather than a server. It works best for short clips at 720p or lower — 5 minutes or under, no higher than 720p, converts in a reasonable amount of time. Longer or higher-resolution recordings take proportionally longer — keep the tab open until it finishes.

Is this free?

Yes, completely free, no account or install required.

Can I record just a portion of my screen instead of a whole tab or window?

Only whatever your browser's own sharing dialog offers — typically a full tab, full window, or full screen. A custom partial-region selection isn't part of that browser API.

Does it capture my microphone too?

It captures tab or system audio, depending on what you share — microphone input isn't included unless your own system audio setup routes it in separately.

What happens if I close the tab mid-recording?

The recording is lost — keep the tab open and active until you've clicked Stop and downloaded the file.

Being straight about this: this records video/audio from a tab, window, or screen you explicitly choose and approve through your browser's own sharing dialog — the same permission prompt used for video calls. It works best in Chrome or Edge on desktop; screen capture isn't supported on most mobile browsers. You're responsible for only recording video you actually have the right to record.
